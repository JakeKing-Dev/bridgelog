Terms of use

Plain language · Updated October 2026

The short version. BridgeLog is free to use during the pilot, through June 30, 2027. Your records stay on your device and belong to you and your school. Use it the way your school's rules allow, and keep your own backups.

Who these terms are between

These terms are between you (and the school or district you work for, if you use BridgeLog for work) and Jacob King, who makes BridgeLog. Using the app means you accept them.

Cost

BridgeLog is free for pilot schools through June 30, 2027, and one classroom stays free after that. School and district licenses start with the 2027–28 school year. Nobody will be charged without signing up for a license, and the logs you've made never lock, whatever you decide.

Your records

  • Everything you log is stored in your browser on your device. It isn't sent to BridgeLog or anyone else, and the maker can't see it.
  • Your records belong to you and your school. Your school decides how they're kept, shared and removed, and how long they're kept.
  • Because records exist only on your device, you're responsible for backups. Clearing the browser's data, losing the device or uninstalling the browser can erase them. BridgeLog reminds you to save backups; please do.

Using it at school

  • Check your school's rules before logging real students, and follow them, including rules about devices, student records and privacy.
  • Use initials or first names, as the app asks. Don't enter diagnoses, disability categories or full IEP text.
  • BridgeLog is a record-keeping tool. It doesn't give medical, behavioral or legal advice. Staff follow each student's plans (seizure action plans, behavior plans, IEPs) and district procedures, and decisions belong to the student's team.

The software

  • BridgeLog is provided as it is, and it's still being improved. It's tested carefully, but no software is free of mistakes, so check what you print before you rely on it.
  • To the extent the law allows, the maker isn't liable for lost records or for decisions made using the app. Nothing here limits rights you have under the law that can't be limited.
  • The source code is public so your IT staff can review it. Reading it is welcome; it isn't licensed for copying or resale.
  • These terms may be updated. The date at the top shows the latest version, and a change that affects your records or what you pay will be posted here first.
